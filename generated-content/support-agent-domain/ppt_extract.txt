What is CoSell?
TEXT:
The business problem it solves and who depends on it
TEXT:
The Business Problem
Microsoft partners (ISVs, SIs, resellers) co-sell Microsoft products alongside their own solutions. Tracking whether a partner referral turned into a real opportunity, whether revenue was recognized, and how fast deals progressed through the funnel requires combining data from multiple disconnected systems (Partner Center, MSX/CRM, currency tables, account hierarchies).

What CoSell Does
CoSell unifies this data into a single analytical model that answers:
  •  Did a partner referral convert into a Microsoft sales opportunity?
  •  How fast did Microsoft respond (inbound velocity)?
  •  What is the deal worth in USD across 9 different financial measures?
  •  Was the partner's CRM account co-sell-ready (ISV eligible)?
  •  Which scorecard month gets fiscal credit for this deal?
  •  What is the partner's quantitative impact on Microsoft's business (PIN)?

Who Depends on CoSell
GPS Leadership, Partner Development Managers, Finance (revenue attribution), Field Sellers (scorecard credit), ISV Program (co-sell readiness tracking), CoMarketing (investment ROI).
TEXT:
80+ Gold TablesDimensional model covering the full deal lifecycle
TEXT:
9 USD Financial MetricsWith sophisticated multi-currency exchange rate logic
TEXT:
12 Performance MetricsVelocity, acceptance, ISV engagement, win rates
TEXT:
5 Source Systems UnifiedPartner Center, MSX/CRM, Hub, Currency, Revenue Account
TEXT:
PIN MetricsPartner Impact Numbers across E5 Security, CSP Copilot, etc.

--- Slide 4 ---
TEXT:
Key Business Terminology
TEXT:
The language of CoSell — terms every stakeholder must know
TEXT:
Co-Sell
TEXT:
A joint selling motion where Microsoft and a partner collaborate to sell to a customer. The partner refers a deal (or Microsoft refers to the partner), and both work together through the sales cycle.
TEXT:
Referral / Partner Deal
TEXT:
A deal initiated in Partner Center by a partner (Outbound from MS perspective) or sent to a partner by Microsoft (Inbound). Each gets a unique PSXDealID. This is the atomic unit of CoSell tracking.
TEXT:
PartnerOne ID
TEXT:
Microsoft's unified partner identity. A single partner may have multiple MPN IDs, but PartnerOne consolidates them into one hierarchy: PartnerOneID → PartnerOneSubKey (subsidiary level) → PartnerOneReportingKey (rolled-up for reporting).
TEXT:
Partner Sales Stage
TEXT:
The lifecycle stage of a deal: Support 100%, Deploy 100%, MS Win 100% (positive closures), or Disengaged 0%, Loss 0% (negative closures). A deal is 'Closed' when it reaches any of these terminal stages.
TEXT:
ISV Co-Sell Ready
TEXT:
A flag on the partner's CRM account (IsCoSellReadyPartnerAccount). Only deals from ISV co-sell-ready partners are counted in ISVDealsReporting. This is an eligibility gate, not a deal attribute.
TEXT:
Partner GTM (Go-To-Market)
TEXT:
A flag (IsDealWithPartnerGTM) indicating the deal participates in Microsoft's Partner GTM program. Used as a prerequisite for IsDealAccepted and IsOpportunityWon calculations.
TEXT:
PIN (Partner Impact Number)
TEXT:
A quantitative measure of partner contribution to Microsoft's business. Metrics like E5 Security Usage and CSP Copilot Seats are per-partner (Non-Aggregate); others can be summed across partners (Aggregate).
TEXT:
ACR (Azure Consumed Revenue)
TEXT:
The actual Azure consumption revenue attributed to partner deals. PartnerReportedACR is self-reported by partners; TrueACRConsumption is system-verified.
TEXT:
Scorecard Recognition Month
TEXT:
The fiscal month a deal gets credited to. Governed by the 3rd-of-month rule: deals approved after the 3rd → current month; before the 3rd → prior month.
TEXT:
ATU (Account Team Unit)
TEXT:
Microsoft's internal sales org structure. Mapped via MSSalesID → ATUID from DimRevenueAccount. Connects deals to the field sales team responsible.

TEXT:
Deal Directions: Inbound vs Outbound
TEXT:
How co-sell deals flow between Microsoft and Partners
TEXT:
INBOUND  (Partner → Microsoft)
TEXT:
The partner identifies a customer opportunity and refers it TO Microsoft through Partner Center.Business meaning: The partner is bringing Microsoft into their deal.Velocity metric: VelocityOfInboundDeals measures how many days it takes Microsoft to create an MSX Opportunity after receiving the partner's referral.Logic from code:  IF Direction = 'Inbound' AND OpportunityCreatedDate IS NOT NULL:    VelocityOfInboundDeals = DATEDIFF(OpportunityCreatedDate, CreatedDate)    (minimum 1 day if same-day)  ELSE: 0 (not applicable)
TEXT:
OUTBOUND  (Microsoft → Partner)
TEXT:
Microsoft identifies an opportunity and refers it TO a partner through Partner Center.Business meaning: Microsoft is inviting a partner to collaborate on their deal.Velocity metric: Not tracked via VelocityOfInboundDeals (= 0 for outbound). Instead, PartnerDealsAcceptance tracks whether the partner accepted the referral.Key flag:  PartnerDealsAcceptance = 1 when:    • OpportunityID exists (deal was linked)    • PartnerDealID is valid    • CRM Partner is known (PartnerName IS NOT NULL)    • PartnerAcceptanceStatus = 'Accepted'
TEXT:
Private deals (ReferralSubType = 'private - pc') have special geography handling: CustomerGeographyKey is forced to 0 and ReportingGeographyKey to -9999. The PartnerOneReportingKey falls back to the Bridge table with SubsidiaryID = -9999.

--- Slide 7 ---
TEXT:
Currency Conversion Logic
TEXT:
The Oct-2018 cutoff rule and multi-source exchange rate fallback strategy
TEXT:
All financial metrics are stored in USD. Because deals originate in local currencies from Partner Center and MSX, the system uses exchange rates from multiple sources with a date-dependent priority order.
TEXT:
THE OCT-2018 RULE:  RegistrationDate < 2018-10-01 → Opportunity rate preferred first  |  RegistrationDate >= 2018-10-01 → Partner Deal rate preferred first
TEXT:
Simple Conversion Metrics (single source)
TEXT:
DealValueCD
TEXT:
DealValue / CurrencyExchangeRate (from Currency table by CurrencyID)
TEXT:
PartnerDealValueCD
TEXT:
PartnerDealValue / rate by PartnerDealCurrencyID
TEXT:
MicrosoftDealValueCD
TEXT:
MicrosoftDealValue / rate by MicrosoftDealCurrencyID
TEXT:
SolutionValueCD
TEXT:
SolutionValue / rate by SolutionCurrencyID
TEXT:
Date-Dependent Fallback Metrics
TEXT:
ProductRevenueCD (Dual fallback)
TEXT:
Pre-Oct 2018: Opportunity rate → Partner Deal rate → 1Post-Oct 2018: Partner Deal rate → Opportunity rate → 1
TEXT:
PartnerRevenueCD (Triple fallback)
TEXT:
Pre-Oct 2018: Opportunity → Partner Deal → Currency table → 1Post-Oct 2018: Partner Deal → Currency table → Opportunity → 1
TEXT:
ContractValueCD (Triple fallback)
TEXT:
Same as PartnerRevenueCD
TEXT:
EstimatedRevenueCD (Dual fallback)
TEXT:
Same as ProductRevenueCD
TEXT:
WHY THIS MATTERS:  The fallback chain ensures no deal has NULL financial values. If the preferred exchange rate source is missing (e.g., Opportunity has no currency code), the system gracefully falls back to the next source. The Oct-2018 cutoff reflects a historical change in how Partner Center recorded currency vs. how MSX did — older deals trust Opportunity rates more; newer deals trust Partner Deal rates.

--- Slide 11 ---
TEXT:
Scorecard Recognition Logic
TEXT:
The 3rd-of-month boundary rule that determines when a deal gets fiscal credit
TEXT:
When a partner deal is approved, it must be credited to a specific fiscal month for scorecard purposes. The recognition month is NOT simply the month of the approval date — a boundary rule applies:
TEXT:
THE 3RD-OF-MONTH RULEIF  DAY(ApprovalDate) > 3   OR   MONTH(ApprovalDate) = MONTH(RegistrationDate)    THEN  ScorecardRecognitionMonth = FORMAT(ApprovalDate, 'MMMM, yyyy')           ← Current monthELSE    THEN  ScorecardRecognitionMonth = FORMAT(ApprovalDate - 1 month, 'MMMM, yyyy')  ← Prior month
TEXT:
Examples:
TEXT:
Deal approved March 15
TEXT:
Day(15) > 3 → TRUE
TEXT:
March recognized
TEXT:
✓ Standard case
TEXT:
Deal approved March 2, registered March 1
TEXT:
Day(2) ≤ 3 BUT same month as registration
TEXT:
March recognized
TEXT:
✓ Same-month exception
TEXT:
Deal approved March 2, registered February 15
TEXT:
Day(2) ≤ 3 AND different month
TEXT:
February recognized
TEXT:
⚠ Pushed back 1 month
TEXT:
Deal approved March 1, registered January 10
TEXT:
Day(1) ≤ 3 AND different month
TEXT:
February recognized
TEXT:
⚠ Pushed back 1 month
TEXT:
WHY THIS EXISTS: Prevents gaming of scorecard timing. Deals approved in the first 3 days of a month (that were registered in a prior month) are likely 'carried over' approvals and should credit the prior month. This aligns with Microsoft's fiscal period close procedures.

Now, once you know what table hold what information, it becomes easy to analyze and see how these tables must be related

This is a standard Star Schema where we the fact table is surrounded and related with the dimension tables around it except the bridge table which is further related for partner related data

All the keys which are there in fact table will have a related dimension and those will be the columns which will be used to take joins while creating the tables

For example, as you can see I want to see the data with AZ pricing level what I will do is that  I have AZ pricing level 2 key and I have the same key in the fact view and what I will do now is that I will join the view based on this key to view the consumption based on AZ pricing level 2 key. 
The next you can see is vw_Dim_AzureFRAGroup And to see the data for Azure FRA group 2 what will have to do is join the view of dimension and the view of fact based on Azure FRA Group Key and the exactly of the column is Dim_AzureFRAGroupID. 
The same thing is needed for all the dimension views which are related directly with the fact view


Then you can see that we have vw_Bridge_AzureConsumptionPartner which is a bridge view to join with the views which are related to dimensions of partner and partner association which are vw_Dim_PartnerAttachType and vw_ReportingPartnerOne. In order to see the data at partner level, we need to join the fact table with the bridge table and the bridge table with either the DimPartnerAttachType or ReportingPartnerOne based on what you are looking at. 


One thing which you must understand is what attributes in a query is additive and what attributes are not additive,
And this is not something which you should memorize, whatever attributes are related directly to the fact table are always additive, and whatever attributes need a bridge table are never additive. That is the reason of adding the bridge tables in the data model

  | One line Definition:
The amount Microsoft invoices customers or CSPs for the Azure IP co-sell offers purchased through Microsoft Commercial Marketplace. |  | Detailed Definition: 
After customers or CSPs purchase Azure IP Co-Sell tagged solutions in Microsoft Commercial Marketplace, Microsoft invoices them. Marketplace Billed Sales will appear only once Microsoft issues an invoice to the customer/CSP. 

The MBS metric includes all Azure IP Co-sell tagged solutions from partners with Top Tier designation that are transactable and where Microsoft invoices the customer.  It includes Azure applications (Virtual Machine Images", "Solution Templates", "SaaS”)

Seller Credit Calculation 
Marketplace Billed Sales annualized value 1x credit for Top Tier with $1M Cap. Non-Top Tier partners will be prompted to achieve Top Tier designation.
Cap per deal applied after modifier for Annualized & Monthly
Sellers receive quota and actuals | Business Objective & Alignment to Solution Area Priority 
Measures the sale of 3rd party IP co-sell tagged solutions that run on Microsoft Cloud, thus growing Azure cloud consumption. Providing sellers with an incentive is a key MCPP benefit for Top Tier designated IP Partners to gain increased opportunities via access to our Managed Account Teams.
Limiting to Top Tier partners incents partners to take action to meet the criteria and join the Top Tier list. | In Scope Products / Workloads
